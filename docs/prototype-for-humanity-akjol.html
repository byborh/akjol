The problem — orientation is the last great inequality

Choosing what to study is among the most consequential decisions a young person makes — and one of the most unequal. A teenager whose parents are doctors or engineers inherits a network, private counselors and insider knowledge. A teenager from a working-class town or a rural area is handed a passive catalog and left to guess.

Existing tools make this worse, each in its own way:

Public catalogs

Onisep, Parcoursup — exhaustive but static. They list options; they never tell you what a choice means for someone in your situation.

Private platforms

Lead-generation engines that rank schools by who pays the most. Advice is structurally biased.

Generative AI chatbots

Fluent but unsourced — they fabricate admission rates, equivalences and outcomes with total confidence.

The human cost

Wasted years, debt, dropouts, forced reconversions — a decision worth tens of thousands of euros made with the least reliable information of one's life.

The solution — a router, not a catalog

AkJol (“White Path” in Kazakh) is an active study & career router. The student enters their starting point — current level, country, languages, budget, visa constraints — and AkJol computes every trajectory genuinely accessible to them, with explicit conditions, calibrated admission probabilities, the exact application procedure, and a Plan B if they are rejected.

“Students don't want to search a catalog — they want to be told honestly where each choice leads, including when it leads nowhere.”

Reverse routing

From a target job, trace back the study paths that reach it from this student's profile. Unique in French.

Radical honesty

Every figure shows its source and confidence interval. No orphan numbers — and where the data is missing, AkJol says “not covered” rather than guessing.

Neutral by design

Organic results are never reordered by payment. No paid ranking, ever.

Four honest statuses — including “I don't know”

The fourth is the one no competitor ships: where data is insufficient, AkJol says so instead of guessing.

● Open — every requirement met today.
● Open with a step — one reachable thing missing (certificate, bridge year, diploma in progress).
● Closed — a structural requirement is missing, and AkJol names which one.
● Not covered — not enough data to judge; never filled with a plausible guess.

Where we honestly stand

The engine is complete and running in production. The catalog is deliberately narrow — France × IT, curated program by program from official sources.

That is a choice, not a shortfall. A product whose entire premise is honesty cannot pad its database with scraped approximations: every uncurated record would surface as a confident-looking answer we cannot stand behind. So the fourth status exists, and the catalog grows only as fast as it can be verified. Breadth is the next phase, and the pipeline that produces it already works — the Parcoursup generator is idempotent, and each new source is a connector, not a rewrite.

Vision

AkJol becomes the francophone “life router”: orientation (15–25), reconversion (25–50), active retirement (50+). Starting with France and the Tech vertical, then expanding to Canada, Belgium, the UK and the US through open data connectors.

The business model stays neutral by construction: institutions may one day pay for aggregated, anonymised usage insight — never to influence what a student is shown. No paid ranking, no lead resale, no advertising. Trust is the only asset that compounds here, and it is the one asset that cannot be bought back once spent.

A student from a forgotten town gets the same quality of guidance as one from the wealthiest district. Give everyone their White Path.
